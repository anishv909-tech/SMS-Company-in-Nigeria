## Promotional and Transactional Messaging

Different messages serve different purposes. Separating promotional campaigns from service-related notifications can make messaging easier to manage.

### Promotional Campaigns

Promotional messages communicate offers, product launches, seasonal campaigns, and other marketing announcements. Businesses can organize campaigns around customer interests, relevant timing, and clearly defined objectives.

For example, a retailer may inform interested customers about a new collection, while a training provider may announce enrollment dates for an upcoming course.

### Transactional Notifications

Transactional messages provide information connected to a customer's activity or an existing service. Examples include booking confirmations, payment acknowledgments, and order-status updates.

These messages should be accurate, relevant, and delivered according to the requirements of the underlying transaction.

## Integrating SMS with Business Applications

An SMS integration can connect messaging functionality to an existing website, mobile application, CRM, or internal business system.

A typical integration process includes:

1. Identify the events that should trigger a message.
2. Review the provider's API documentation and authentication requirements.
3. Configure the sender details and message templates.
4. Validate recipient numbers and required message fields.
5. Test successful submissions, errors, and delivery-status handling.
6. Monitor logs and usage after deployment.

For example, an e-commerce platform may trigger an order confirmation after a successful purchase. The application sends the required information to the messaging service and records the result for operational monitoring.

Businesses should protect API credentials, validate incoming data, and use sensible retry rules to prevent duplicate notifications.

## Campaign Planning and Contact Management

A successful campaign starts with a clear purpose. Before sending a message, define the intended audience, the action recipients should take, and the result that will indicate success.

Good campaign practices include:

* Use contact lists collected through appropriate channels.
* Maintain accurate and updated recipient information.
* Obtain the necessary consent for promotional communications.
* Include clear sender identification where supported.
* Keep message content concise and relevant.
* Respect opt-out requests and applicable messaging rules.
* Schedule messages at appropriate times for the intended audience.

These practices can improve communication quality while reducing unwanted messages and avoidable campaign errors.

## Measuring Messaging Performance

Reporting helps businesses understand how their messaging activity performs. Depending on the platform, reports may provide submission counts, delivery statuses, failures, and usage information.

Useful metrics include:

* Total messages submitted
* Reported deliveries and delivery failures
* Campaign costs
* Responses or conversions, where measurable
* Performance differences between audience segments

Businesses should distinguish between a submitted message and a confirmed delivery. Delivery reports can also vary according to provider capabilities and mobile network behavior.
